# Phase 7 results: independent validation, generalisation, fair comparison

Every number below comes from `results/phase7/*/results.json` via `results/phase7/SUMMARY.md`,
generated by `python scripts/phase7/analyze.py`. Figures are in `results/phase7/figures/`. All
runs used a clean tree. Their FEM, protocol and experiment code is unchanged since commit 64668e7
(see the provenance comment of each section in SUMMARY.md). The hypotheses were stated beforehand
in `PHASE7_RESEARCH_PLAN.md` §5. Caveats are in `PHASE7_LIMITATIONS.md`; novelty is assessed in
`PHASE7_NOVELTY_ASSESSMENT.md`.

**Problem.** Smooth double-Gaussian barrier, s = 0.6, d = 4. Parameters μ = (E, V_L, V_R) in the
box [0.1, 10] × [8, 12]². Out-of-domain (OOD) boxes: heights 12–14 and 6–8. FEM reference:
P1 elements, h = 0.0025, N = 4331.

## Summary

1. **The physics is validated (H1 confirmed).** The FEM converges at O(h²) to an independent ODE
   solution: max |Δt| 9.1e-3 → 8.9e-6 for V = (10, 10) over six meshes, ratio 3.93–4.01 per halving. At the production mesh
   the error is 4e-5 to 2e-4 in t. Resonance energies are within 0.65 % of Γ and widths within
   2e-5 relative. Every resonance is a pole that is stable under mesh refinement, domain growth
   and energy-grid refinement; no artefacts were found.
2. **Projection RB generalises; data-driven surrogates do not (H2 confirmed against the
   baselines).** With three parameters (E, V_L, V_R; the space is one-dimensional), Galerkin RB reaches max |Δt| = 1.2e-9 on 1024 held-out points with 48 FEM
   solves, and 5.7e-4 with 24. RBF on t, POD regression and the MLP stay at max |Δt| ≈ 0.8–1.4
   at every budget, the MLP up to 4096 training points. The audit showed the MLP was under-trained:
   tuned on validation data (256 × 3, 10k epochs) it reaches a mean |ΔT| of 2.6–3.5e-3 instead of
   9.1e-3, but its worst case stays 0.39–0.63, near narrow resonances, where it misses even its own
   training points (`docs/audit/ML_EVALUATION_AUDIT.md`). None of them resolves a single held-out
   resonance.
3. **Greedy sampling is not uniformly better than a Sobol design (contrary to a natural
   expectation).** Max |Δt| is similar for greedy and Sobol RB at n ≥ 20 (n = 24: 5.7e-4 vs
   6.3e-4; n = 32: 3.9e-6 vs 8.7e-6). Greedy helps the resonance metrics at small n: at n = 16
   recall is 1.00 vs 0.61. Galerkin is more accurate than LSPG at every budget.
4. **Extrapolation (H3).** RB loses about a factor of 3 OOD at intermediate budgets (n = 24:
   2.0e-3 vs 5.7e-4) and nothing at n = 48 (8.0e-10). The MLP's mean error (original, untuned configuration) grows 13× OOD (MAE of
   T 9.1e-3 → 0.16).
5. **Basis transfer works better than expected (H4 partly refuted), but saves nothing unless
   rectangular snapshots already exist.** A basis built only from rectangular-barrier snapshots
   reaches max |Δt| = 1.2e-4 on the Gaussian test set with 48 snapshots, which is the FEM's own
   discretisation error, and 1.6e-5 with 96. A rectangular snapshot costs the same FEM solve as a
   Gaussian one, and a native Gaussian basis reaches 3.9e-6 with 32 and 1.2e-9 with 48 solves, so
   computing rectangular snapshots for transfer is never cheaper here. Transfer pays only when a
   rectangular snapshot set is already available: then 0 Gaussian solves give 1.2e-4, and 8 give
   1.5e-7 (audit correction, P1-B3).
6. **Basis size tracks the number of resonances, not their width (H5 confirmed).** At fixed
   geometry, every additional resonance in the energy window costs about 1.5–3 basis functions.
   Making the narrowest resonance 160× narrower costs at most one. The attainable accuracy in
   double precision is about 5e-11/Γ_min (constant within a factor of 3 across 19 geometries).
7. **The residual/σ_min a-posteriori bound always held and is loose near resonances (H6 confirmed).**
   It is exact in exact arithmetic and was evaluated in floating point (σ_min with a heuristic
   safety margin, the residual without a rounding bound), so it is not a computer-verified
   certificate; the data call it `certified` (audit correction, P1-CERT). Each evaluation needs a
   full-order eigenproblem (≈ 2 s, i.e. thousands of FEM solves). The bound
   res/σ_min held on 100 % of 326 points × 4 bases. It overestimates ‖e_ψ‖ by a median 28–57× on
   random points and 95–400× within ±2Γ of resonances; for t the factors are 10³–10⁵. The
   power-iteration estimate of ‖A⁻¹‖ is up to 3.4 % too small, so it cannot serve as a bound. The
   audit re-checked the bound down to the round-off floor (bases of 24–60 functions, errors down to
   3e-11): it held with a margin of at least 22× (`tests/audit`).
8. **One energy line.** RB reaches the floor (about 7e-8) with 16 queries per scan, adaptive AAA
   with 24 and uniform AAA with about 48. PCHIP fails at all budgets up to 64. This ordering is
   expected, because Galerkin RB on a line is itself a rational function of k.
9. **Robustness.**
   - Under noise, AAA on a uniform grid degrades fastest: 1e-10 noise gives 4e-4 error, against
     3e-6 noise-free. Adaptive AAA is the most noise-tolerant: error ≈ 20–70 × σ. RB built from
     noisy snapshots gives ≈ 400–800 × σ.
   - Overlapping resonance doublets (splitting 0.16 Γ) are resolved by RB at every budget and by
     adaptive AAA from n = 24.
   - Coarse-mesh snapshots projected on the fine operator improve RB only marginally (0.17 vs 0.20
     at h = 0.02).
10. **Cost.** RB is not a speed-up for small workloads. Counting the greedy, break-even against
    the FEM is 2.4e3–1.6e4 queries, or 270–570 with a Sobol design. One-at-a-time ODE and
    staircase S-matrix solves are much slower than the FEM, so their break-even points are small,
    but that mostly reflects per-call Python overhead.

*Audit correction (2026-10-23).* These MLP rows come from a small search (width ≤ 128, 3,000
epochs) and understate the MLP. A frozen, validation-only diagnostic (`results/phase7/mlp_diagnostic/`)

its own training points on the peaks by 0.40–0.56. Concentrating half the data at the resonances
(an oracle design) reduces the worst case only to 0.28–0.44. RBF on the same 4,096 points: mean
2.2e-2. The qualitative conclusion is unchanged.

## D. Basis size

| sweep (one knob at a time, fixed heights) | resonances in window | Γ_min | greedy n for max abs Δt < 1e-4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| V0 = 6 → 18 | 4 → 3 | 5.1e-3 → 3.2e-5 | 13, 12, 12, 12, 12 |
| E_max = 3 → 20 (fixed geometry) | 1 → 5 | 6.9e-4 (fixed) | 7, 9, 12, 16, 17 |
| d = 2 → 8 | 1 → 7 | 1e-1 → 1.9e-5 | 10, 11, 12, 16, 17 |
| s = 0.3 → 0.9 | 4 → 3 | 1.1e-2 → 1.1e-4 | 12, 11, 12, 14 |

A descriptive fit over the 19 configurations gives n(1e-4) ≈ 6.8 + 1.5·N_res + 0.18·log10(1/Γ_min),
with R² = 0.72. Width matters through precision, not basis size: the smallest reachable error is
≈ 5e-11/Γ_min (2–7e-11 × 1/Γ_min over all configurations). Tolerances of 1e-6 and 1e-8 are
therefore unreachable in double precision for Γ_min below roughly 5e-5 and 4e-3 respectively.

- The residual ranks errors well on random points. It ranks them poorly within ±2Γ of resonances,
  where 1/σ_min varies by orders of magnitude.
- The bound is an a-posteriori certificate in the Euclidean nodal norm, with σ_min computed at
  full order. It is not an online-efficient certified-RB bound, and it is not computer-verified
  (no rounding analysis of the residual; heuristic eigensolver margin).

## F. Robustness

Two test lines, n = 32, 5 seeds.

| relative noise σ | AAA uniform | AAA adaptive | RB, noisy snapshots |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (reference) | 3.1e-6 / 6.0e-6 | 6.5e-8 / 4.8e-8 | 7.5e-8 / 3.4e-8 |
| 1e-10 | 4.2e-4 / 2.3e-4 | 1.1e-7 / 3.2e-8 | 6.7e-8 / 3.6e-8 |
| 1e-8 | 9.1e-3 / 7.4e-3 | 6.2e-7 / 2.0e-7 | 3.8e-6 / 5.5e-6 |
| 1e-6 | 0.45 / 0.19 | 6.6e-5 / 1.8e-5 | 7.6e-4 / 2.0e-4 |
| 1e-4 | 0.95 / 0.93 | 1.4e-2 / 0.90 | 0.94 / 0.91 |

The values are the median of max |Δt| on line 1 / line 2. The noise-free row is the n = 32 row of
the 1-D study.

- Energy-grid jitter of ±0.5 spacing: AAA reaches ≤ 3e-5 (worst case), PCHIP still fails, and RB
  on the same jittered design stays at the floor.
- Coarse training data (h = 0.02 / 0.01 / 0.005): the FEM's own error dominates. AAA inherits it
  exactly. RB projected on the fine operator removes a fraction: 0.17 vs 0.20, 3.4e-2 vs 5.0e-2,
  equal at 0.005.
- Doublets: a symmetric triple Gaussian with the middle height 8 → 30 changes the closest pair's
  splitting from 8.6 Γ to 0.16 Γ. RB greedy is at the floor (≈ 1e-8) for every n ≥ 16. Adaptive
  AAA gets there from n = 24, uniform AAA from n = 48.
- Single-precision online solve: 1.7e-6 and 4.0e-7, against 7.5e-8 and 3.4e-8 in double; the
  maximum condition number of the reduced matrix is 5e6.

## What changed relative to the plan

- **Not in the plan:** the round-off floor ∝ 1/Γ_min, found while analysing D. Because of it,
  some tolerance targets cannot be met for a numerical reason, not a basis-size reason.
- **Expected, not observed:** greedy sampling was expected to beat a Sobol design; it does so only
  for the resonance metrics at small n.
- **Expected, not observed:** H4 expected the rectangular basis to transfer poorly; it transfers
  well, at about twice the basis size.
