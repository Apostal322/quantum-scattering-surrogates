# Phase 6 novelty assessment

**Bottom line.** Phase 6 contains no globally new method and no discovery. Its value is an
independent, reproducible, leakage-controlled comparison on a problem where the project already
has exact reference solutions. The comparison sets projection-based reduced-basis (RB) solvers
against the data-driven surrogates of Phases 1–5, and it yields one physically interpretable
observation (category F), which still needs checking against the literature before any claim of
originality.

Numbers below come from `results/phase6/SUMMARY.md` (generated by `scripts/phase6/analyze.py`).

## A. Established physical principles (textbook; not ours)

- 1D stationary scattering with ħ²/2m = 1; flux conservation |t|² + |r|² = 1; transparent (Robin)
  boundary conditions for V = 0 outside a finite interval.
- Resonances are S-matrix poles on the unphysical sheet. Near an isolated pole, the solution is a
  smooth background plus a pole term whose *spatial shape* (the quasi-bound state) depends only weakly
  on energy (Breit–Wigner / Mittag-Leffler structure).
- Galerkin finite elements converge as O(h²) for P1 elements.

## B. Previously published algorithms (used, not invented)

- P1 finite elements; banded LU.
- Reduced-basis methods: POD/SVD bases; Galerkin and least-squares Petrov–Galerkin (LSPG)
  projection; offline/online splitting for affine operators; the residual-norm error indicator;
  the error bound ‖e‖ ≤ ‖A(μ)⁻¹‖‖res‖; the weak greedy algorithm (Hesthaven, Rozza & Stamm 2016;
  Sen et al. 2006; Huynh et al. 2007).
- RB emulators for scattering in nuclear physics, including Galerkin projection and greedy/active
  learning: Furnstahl et al. 2020; Melendez et al. 2021; Drischler et al. 2023; Gnech et al.
  arXiv:2511.01844; Catacora-Rios et al. arXiv:2512.08097; Jin Lei arXiv:2512.17687.
- AAA rational approximation (Nakatsukasa, Sète & Trefethen 2018) and its greedy variant (after
  Pradovera et al.), already in Phases 1–5.
- Thin-plate RBF interpolation; power iteration for ‖A⁻¹‖.

## C. Independent implementations in this repository

- `qscatter/phase6/fom.py`: affine P1-FEM scattering solver with exact transparent boundary
  conditions, validated against the exact S-matrix solver. Second-order convergence and the same
  global phase of t are tested.
- `qscatter/phase6/rb.py`: Galerkin and LSPG reduced models. The residual is evaluated in a thin-QR
  representation of the residual space. A first Gram-matrix version had a round-off floor near
  1e-7 that stalled the greedy loop; this defect was found and fixed during Phase 6. Plus the weak
  residual greedy.
- `qscatter/phase6/baselines.py`: POD coefficient regression, direct t interpolation (RBF, AAA,
  PCHIP) on the same FOM solves, and the inverse-norm estimate.

These are independent implementations of known methods, not contributions in themselves.

## D. Reproduced published results

**None.** No external dataset could be retrieved (`PHASE6_DATA_AUDIT.md`), and the RB papers treat
nuclear systems (p–d, n+48Ca, d+58Ni) whose high-fidelity solvers and data are not available here.
We reproduce *qualitative* behaviour that the RB literature reports in general: rapid error decay
with a few snapshots, and greedy selection beating uniform sampling. This is consistency, not
reproduction of any specific published number.

## E. New results *within our numerical experiments* (measured; see SUMMARY.md)

1. **1D, Stage B (parameter E):** RB-Galerkin with residual greedy reaches the FOM round-off floor
   (max |ΔT| ≈ 6e-9 on a 25 076-point resonance-resolved scan) with **10** FOM solves. AAA on t(k)
   needs **20** with its own greedy rule and more than 30 on a uniform grid. PCHIP and POD
   coefficient regression fail at every budget tested (max |ΔT| of order 1).
2. **3D, (E, V_L, V_R):** RB-Galerkin reaches max |Δt| ≈ 2.3e-9 on 2048 held-out random points
   and max |ΔT| ≈ 7e-9 on 6 held-out resonance-resolved scans (all 18 resonances resolved) with
   **24** FOM solves, for any sampling design (greedy, Sobol, random). Residual greedy helps only at
   intermediate budgets (n = 16: 1.1e-5 vs 8.7e-5 for Sobol). A 4×4×4 tensor grid is worse (9e-4 at
   64 solves). Thin-plate RBF on t and POD coefficient regression on the same solves stay at errors
   of order 1 up to 64 solves.
3. **Residual as an error indicator** (256 held-out points): the Spearman correlation with the true
   wave-function error is 0.84–0.99, and with |Δt| 0.66–0.82. The bound ‖A⁻¹‖‖res‖ ≥ ‖Δψ‖ holds on
   100 % of points but overestimates by a median 440–1100× (up to 7e4×). ‖A⁻¹‖ (median 485, max 1.6e4)
   grows near resonances, as theory predicts.
4. **Basis size:** the numerical rank of dense snapshot sets is nearly **independent of resonance
   width** (Γ_min from 3e-2 to 6e-4 at three resonances: rank 13 → 11 at 1e-10). It **grows by about
   one per additional resonance** (2 → 8 resonances: rank 11 → 18).
5. **Negative results:** POD coefficient regression is unusable for this resonant problem at every
   budget; LSPG is less accurate than Galerkin at small bases in 1D; the comparison with the exact
   S-matrix solver is unfavourable for any surrogate (Section G).

## F. Potential contributions not established as globally novel

- **Observation E4**, with its mechanistic reading: each quasi-bound state adds roughly one spatial
  mode to the solution manifold, and its *width* changes how fast the coefficients vary, not the
  subspace. This explains why projection methods (which need the subspace) are insensitive to
  resonance sharpness, while interpolation of coefficients or observables (which needs the
  coefficient functions) is not.
  - **Close prior work that may already contain this:** eigenvector-continuation and RB emulators for
    resonances and bound states (Furnstahl et al. 2020; Drischler et al. 2023 and references there);
    n-width analyses for Helmholtz-type problems; Mittag-Leffler / quasi-normal-mode expansions of
    resonant scattering.
  - **Status:** a plausible, numerically supported interpretation for this 1D family, not a theorem.
    A literature search with full-text access is required before calling it new.
- **The equal-budget comparison** of projection RB vs AAA (the strongest data-driven 1D method of
  Phases 1–5) on a resonance-resolved, leakage-controlled benchmark. Useful for the coursework; most
  likely not of general interest beyond it.

## G. Claims that need independent verification (or must not be made)

- **No runtime acceleration of a real coupled-channel solver is claimed.** For piecewise-constant 1D
  potentials, the exact transfer/S-matrix solver costs about a microsecond per energy (vectorised),
  far cheaper than both the FEM FOM and RB online evaluation (Cost table in SUMMARY.md). RB is
  only faster than *our FEM FOM*. Any speed-up statement depends on that baseline.
- RB here is **intrusive**: it needs the discretised operator and wave functions. AAA and RBF need
  only t from a black box. The FOM-solve budget comparison does not price that difference.
- Results are for one family of 1D double barriers. Generalisation to coupled channels, Coulomb
  asymptotics, or non-affine parameters (barrier widths and positions, which would need EIM or
  geometry mappings) is untested.
- The CO2–He multi-fidelity question in the Phase 6 brief remains **open and untested**.
